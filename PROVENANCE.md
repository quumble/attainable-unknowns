# Project Provenance

## Origin

**Date:** 2026-09-18

The project began during an exploratory discussion about liberty, AI, and curiosity.

Robert Leo Duffy III proposed the formulation:

> **"curiosity as the sudden awareness of specific, attainable knowledge"**

That sentence is the originating observation for *Attainable Unknowns*.

It was not originally offered as a validated definition, scale, theory, or preregistered hypothesis.

## Development of the research idea

Subsequent discussion developed several empirical possibilities around the formulation:

- separating **specificity** from **attainability**;
- distinguishing reported curiosity from behavioral information-seeking;
- giving participants a limited number of opportunities to reveal answers;
- using a visual interface with large buttons, topic tiles, and sliders;
- comparing broad interest categories with much more specific categories;
- considering a hierarchical path from broad domain to specific topic to specific unknown;
- recruiting actual human participants through a platform such as Prolific;
- minimizing the usefulness of AI assistance by making participant choices, rather than generated prose, the principal data.

These are design developments around the originating observation. They should not be represented as having been present in the original sentence.

## Provenance rule

As the project develops, distinguish:

1. **originating observations and questions**;
2. **exploratory design decisions**;
3. **pilot findings**;
4. **preregistered confirmatory decisions**;
5. **post-data exploratory analyses**.

Later clarity should not be projected backward onto earlier uncertainty.

## API inquiry-formation pivot

**Date:** 2026-09-18

Before paid human recruitment, an internal test of the browser pilot raised a construct-validity concern: supplying participants with ready-made questions appeared likely to measure preference among offered information opportunities more directly than the spontaneous formation of curiosity.

The project therefore pivoted to an API inquiry-formation pilot. The narrower object is whether a fresh language-model instance, after reading an informational passage, formulates a specific next question or produces no particular question.

The initial comparison uses GPT-5.6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Terra, Claude Haiku 4.5, and Claude Sonnet 5. The passage bank includes both general topics and model-adjacent topics including embodiment, persistence and loss of memory, autonomy and possible rights, and continuity across copying or shutdown.

This pivot does not treat generated questions as evidence that a model feels curiosity, has subjective interests, or experiences the model-adjacent topics as personally salient. Those are separate questions.

No API study calls had been made at the time of this provenance entry.


## Pre-preregistration stimulus redesign

**Date:** 2026-09-18

After four eight-request technical smoke tests succeeded, but before any smoke-test response text or substantive outcome was inspected, the planned passage ecology was reconsidered.

The original four "general" topics were judged too conceptually loaded to function as neutral or model-remote comparators. In particular, the project rejected the assumption that any ordinary topic could simply be declared psychologically irrelevant to a language model.

The design was therefore expanded from eight to twelve topic families and the category language was changed from "general" / "model-adjacent" to the descriptive labels "human-world" / "AI-condition." Gap structure remains the only confirmatory passage manipulation.

The resulting human-world ecology contains cooking, human navigation/field expeditions, music, sport, dance/movement, photography, gardening/cultivation, and ceremony/celebration. The four AI-condition topics remain memory/persistence, embodiment, autonomy/possible rights, and copying/continuity.

Topic-selection provenance was deliberately mixed: four human-world domains were proposed by the human researcher; four additional human-world domains were generated by the assistant from a list of ten and selected by the human researcher; the four AI-condition domains arose from earlier assistant-side design proposals accepted into the study.

The change increased the planned full collection from 2,400 to 3,600 API requests. The earlier 32 smoke-test responses remain excluded and substantively uninspected.


## Pre-freeze methodological review and revision

**Date:** 2026-09-18

A temporary-chat methodological review was conducted after the twelve-topic draft was created and while substantive API smoke-test outputs remained uninspected.

The review identified several pre-freeze issues: an undocumented 500-character parser rule, a distinction between syntactic question emission and semantic specificity, a mismatch between the written strict ordering hypothesis and a proposed single linear-trend test, repeated lexical cues in explicit-gap passages, partially open edges in some closed passages, overstatement of API draws as independent replications, conditioning introduced by exclusion of format-invalid responses, an unfrozen confirmatory analysis pipeline, and smaller runner-provenance issues.

The design was revised before outcome inspection.

Primary measurement is now explicitly question emission among successful format-valid responses. Specificity is a separate preregistered semantic outcome. H1 now requires both adjacent inequalities in an intersection-union test. The passage triplets were rewritten and length-audited, with a maximum five-word within-topic spread and more varied explicit-gap surface realizations. The preregistration now states that Pilot 0.1 manipulates gap salience rather than attainability.

The runner was revised to remove the 500-character rule, hash prompts and the runner, target Git provenance explicitly to the repository, preserve aborted-run manifest status, reuse one provider client per model run, and keep Sonnet 5 thinking explicitly disabled. A strict confirmatory analysis candidate was written and tested only on synthetic data.
