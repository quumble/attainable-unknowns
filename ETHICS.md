# Human Participant Baseline

**Status: working ethics protocol for a proposed minimal-risk study.**

Prolific or another recruitment platform can provide identity checks, recruitment infrastructure, payment handling, and platform-level participant protections. Those services do **not** transfer responsibility for the study design, consent process, data minimization, or treatment of participants.

## Baseline for the first study

The first study should be deliberately simple:

- adults only;
- informed consent before study activity;
- fair, transparent compensation;
- no deceptive cover story unless separately justified and reviewed;
- no distressing or intentionally provocative material;
- no medical, legal, political, sexual, traumatic, or other sensitive questioning unless a later design specifically requires it;
- no unnecessary demographic collection;
- no direct identifiers requested by the study;
- clear ability to stop participation;
- genuine answers provided when participants spend a reveal.

## Consent

Consent language should explain, in plain language:

- that the task studies information preference and information-seeking;
- the approximate task length;
- what participants will do;
- what data are recorded;
- foreseeable risks, if any;
- compensation;
- voluntary participation and withdrawal;
- how study data will be stored and used;
- researcher contact information appropriate to the recruitment platform.

The exact confirmatory hypothesis need not be disclosed in advance if doing so would invalidate the study, provided the description remains truthful and non-deceptive.

## Data minimization

Collect the minimum needed to answer the study question.

The first design does not inherently require names, email addresses, physical addresses, political identity, race, income, health information, or other sensitive demographics.

Platform participant identifiers, if received, should be treated as potentially identifying research data even when they are pseudonymous.

Raw participant exports should not be committed to this repository by default. See [data/README.md](data/README.md).

## AI contamination

The preferred defense against AI-assisted responding is task design, not speculative AI detection.

The study should emphasize:

- button and tile choices;
- sliders;
- limited reveal allocation;
- navigation choices;
- prediction or revision behavior where appropriate;
- minimal optional free text.

If AI-detection or platform authenticity signals are later used for exclusions, the rule should be specified prospectively and should not be treated as infallible evidence of misconduct.

## Deception

The initial study should not require deception.

Any later study using deception should separately address:

- why deception is necessary;
- whether the same question can be answered without it;
- debriefing;
- participant withdrawal after debriefing where appropriate;
- recruitment-platform requirements;
- whether independent ethics or IRB review is required.

## Publication and review

A minimal-risk design is not automatically exempt from every institutional, journal, legal, or ethical requirement.

Before public dissemination, confirm the requirements applicable to the intended venue and the status of the researcher. Do not retrospectively describe a study as IRB-approved, exempt, or independently reviewed unless that determination actually occurred.
