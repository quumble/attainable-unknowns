# API Pilot 0.1 — Exploratory Semantic Target Protocol E1 Amendment E1A

**Study:** Attainable Unknowns API Pilot 0.1  
**Protocol:** Exploratory Semantic Target Protocol E1  
**Amendment ID:** E1A  
**Status:** Final candidate; adopted only by the founder-signed commit containing this file  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-19  
**E1 adoption commit:** `747cd60f54a2c2bdd20bcd86cc8f9d9b94bb9d34`  
**Frozen Stage 1 corpus checkpoint:** `15dbac07a615938464b03f5fa3e2539aed3ef8c1`  
**Stage:** After Stage 1 packet generation; before any study card receives a Stage 1 canonical target  
**Scope:** Dual independent Stage 1 semantic representations and parallel downstream analysis

## 1. Purpose

E1 originally specifies one blinded canonical-target representation for each of the
1,984 unique visible Stage 1 cards.

E1A changes that procedure to produce **two complete, independent Stage 1
canonical-target representations per card**, one from an OpenAI-family
canonicalizer and one from an Anthropic-family canonicalizer.

The purpose is to preserve representation-model dependence as an observable
robustness dimension rather than treating one model-produced semantic
representation as authoritative.

E1A does not alter the frozen corpus, exact-visible duplicate collapse,
semantic-target definition, Stage 1 visible fields, target-equivalence rule,
Stage 2 partition rule, concentration measures, focal-gap alignment procedure,
mechanical companion analyses, or interpretive boundaries of E1.

## 2. Timing and prior knowledge

E1A is adopted, if at all, after:

- the original response corpus was collected and analyzed;
- E1 was adopted;
- the deterministic Stage 1 corpus was generated and anchored; and
- the founder and preparing assistant discussed using two canonicalizer families.

At the time E1A was prepared, **no study card had received a Stage 1 canonical
target** and no Stage 2 semantic partition had been produced.

E1A remains part of a post hoc exploratory analysis. Its adoption does not make
E1 or E1A confirmatory.

## 3. Fixed Stage 1 corpus

E1A uses the already-frozen E1 Stage 1 packet without regeneration or resampling.

The corpus remains:

- 2,919 eligible emitted-question observations;
- 1,984 unique exact passage + question cards;
- 935 duplicate observations represented through card multiplicity;
- 12 topic families.

The frozen Stage 1 corpus checkpoint is:

`15dbac07a615938464b03f5fa3e2539aed3ef8c1`

No E1A procedure may alter card IDs, card contents, multiplicities, topic
membership, or the private metadata mapping.

## 4. Dual Stage 1 canonicalizers

Every one of the 1,984 frozen cards is canonicalized independently by both:

1. **OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol**
2. **Anthropic Claude Opus 5**

This produces **3,968 planned Stage 1 semantic-coding requests** in total.

The exact served API model identifiers, provider settings, timestamps, execution
route, and any provider-exposed reasoning or thinking configuration must be
recorded in the execution record.

Neither canonicalizer is one of the four model products that generated the
Pilot 0.1 response corpus. The original corpus was generated by GPT-5.6 Luna,
GPT-5.6 Terra, Claude Haiku 4.5, and Claude Sonnet 5.

This separation reduces direct same-product self-coding but does not establish
independence from provider family, training lineage, or shared conventions.

## 5. Independence and blinding

Each canonicalizer receives exactly one card per request.

Each request contains only:

- the frozen Stage 1 canonicalization instructions;
- `card_id`;
- `passage`;
- `question`.

No request may contain:

- the other canonicalizer's output for that card;
- another study card;
- model identity of the response generator;
- provider identity of the response generator;
- gap label;
- topic label or topic ID;
- card multiplicity;
- run metadata;
- specificity result;
- primary outcome;
- aggregate E1 result.

No conversational state is carried from one card to another.

The two canonicalizers are not shown one another's outputs before both complete
representations are frozen.

## 6. Stage 1 outputs

For each card and each representation family, the required output remains exactly:

- `card_id`;
- `canonical_target`;
- `uncertain`.

The frozen Stage 1 instructions and semantic-target definition remain unchanged.

The two complete output sets are stored separately as the **Sol representation**
and the **Opus representation**.

Neither representation is treated as authoritative.

The two representations are not merged, averaged, reconciled, adjudicated, or
collapsed into a consensus representation before Stage 2.

A technical failure, malformed response, or noncompletion is handled according to
the signed Stage 1 runner procedure. An output may not be discarded or replaced
because its semantic content appears unusual or because it disagrees with the
other representation.

## 7. Stage 2 parallelization

Stage 2 is performed separately on the two complete representation sets.

For each of the 12 opaque topic packets:

- the Sol-derived canonical targets form one Stage 2 input set;
- the Opus-derived canonical targets form a second Stage 2 input set.

The original E1 requirement for three independent partitioning instances applies
separately to each representation.

Accordingly, the planned Stage 2 structure contains:

- 12 topic packets × 3 partition replicates for the Sol representation; and
- 12 topic packets × 3 partition replicates for the Opus representation.

Partitioners receive only the canonical-target representation assigned to that
Stage 2 run and the fields permitted by E1. They are not told the model/gap
metadata of the original observations.

No partition from one representation is used to alter or repair a partition from
the other representation.

## 8. Downstream reporting

All E1 semantic results that depend on Stage 1 representation are reported
separately under the Sol-derived and Opus-derived representations.

This includes, as applicable:

- target-cluster counts;
- top-target share;
- Shannon target entropy;
- effective number of targets;
- Simpson concentration;
- singleton/rare-target prevalence;
- cross-model Jensen-Shannon divergence;
- focal-gap alignment;
- unique-card sensitivity;
- observation-weighted results;
- Stage 2 partition stability.

Agreement in substantive pattern across the two representation families is an
exploratory robustness result.

Disagreement is also an exploratory result and must remain visible. One
representation may not be selected as the preferred result merely because it
produces clearer gap differences, stronger convergence, or a more appealing
interpretation.

No post hoc reconciliation of the two representations is required by E1A.

## 9. Preservation boundary

Before Stage 2 begins, preserve for both representation families:

- complete raw provider outputs;
- validated parsed Stage 1 outputs;
- provider/model/settings metadata;
- input packet and instruction hashes;
- execution records;
- retry/correction/noncompletion records;
- complete card coverage checks.

Both completed Stage 1 representation sets must be frozen before either is used
to create Stage 2 semantic partitions.

The private E1 metadata key remains outside all semantic-coder inputs and is not
joined to Stage 1 or Stage 2 semantic outputs until the blind semantic bundle
required by E1 is preserved.

## 10. Adoption and deviations

E1A becomes operative only through the founder-signed commit containing this
file.

Its adoption timestamp must precede the first real Stage 1 canonicalization
request.

Any material departure from E1A after adoption is preserved as a subsequent
amendment or deviation record rather than silently rewriting E1A.

Nothing in E1A changes the exploratory status of the analysis.
